Guide & FAQ

Getting started

How do I get started?
Create your account with an email and password (a quick “verify you’re human” check may appear). A short tour then points out the main buttons — you can replay it any time with the button at the top of this Guide tab. After that, open Settings and: pick your currency, choose how you’re paid (salary or wages), enter your income, and set a budget for each category. Everything else updates automatically as you log expenses. Each new account starts with 16 ready-made categories you can rename, re-budget, add to or remove.
What’s the difference between “Salary” and “Wages”?
Salary means a fixed payday each month (you choose the day, 1–28; the default is the 25th). Your cycle runs from one payday to the next, and the Dashboard shows the days left and your safe-to-spend-per-day. Wages means no fixed schedule: your cycle starts when you start it and runs until you end it yourself with “Archive this cycle & start fresh” in Settings. Nothing is archived automatically, and because there’s no end date to measure against, the Dashboard shows “Cycle day” instead of a countdown, pace badge or per-day allowance. You can switch in Settings at any time; switching to Wages starts a new open-ended cycle from today.
Why does my payday move around? (Salary)
Your payday is a fixed day of the month. If it lands on a Saturday or Sunday, the cycle shifts to the Friday before, which is when most salaries are actually paid.
Which currencies can I use, and what happens if I change mine?
Settings → Currency offers about 85 currencies, including Naira, US, Canadian and Australian dollars, pound, euro, rand, cedi and many more across Africa, the Americas, Europe, the Middle East and Asia. Changing it changes the symbol shown on every amount — it does not convert numbers you’ve already entered. In PDF reports, symbols the PDF font can’t draw (such as ₦) are printed as the 3-letter code (NGN) instead.
Can I use the app on my phone like a normal app?
Yes. Open it in your phone’s browser and choose “Add to Home Screen” (or “Install app”). It then opens full-screen like any other app.

Logging your money

How do I log an expense?
Tap the + button, enter the amount, pick a category, and add a note, a payment method and a date if you like. The date defaults to today. Tick “Repeats every cycle” to have it logged again automatically at the start of each new cycle (rent, subscriptions).
How do I edit or delete an expense I logged wrong?
Tap any transaction to edit it — amount, category, method, note and date can all be changed, and Save updates it in place instead of creating a duplicate. On a phone you can also swipe a row left for quick Edit and Delete buttons, and the ✕ button deletes it directly.
What are payment methods for, and do I have to use them?
They’re optional. Tagging an expense as Cash, Card, USSD, Mobile Wallet or Bank Transfer doesn’t change any calculation — it just shows in your transaction list and exports so you can spot patterns such as “I overspend when paying by card”.
How do I add extra income, including money in another currency?
Tap “+ Add extra income”. Pick the currency you actually received it in and the app looks up a live exchange rate (from Frankfurter, a free service that collects rates from central banks and other official sources) and shows a preview like “≈ ₦77,500 at today’s rate” before you save. The original amount, currency, rate and date are kept on the entry, and only the converted home-currency amount counts toward your budget. If the live rate can’t be fetched (no connection, or an unusual currency), enter the amount in your home currency instead.
What does “Safe to spend per day” mean?
It’s (remaining budget) ÷ (days left until payday) — a pace check. Spending at or below it each remaining day lands you within budget by payday; going over on one day just means tighter days ahead, not an emergency. It only applies to Salary; Wages cycles have no end date, so it isn’t shown.
Why aren’t Savings and Emergency Fund counted as spending?
Money you move into savings isn’t an expense — it’s still yours, just set aside. It’s left out of “Spent” and tracked on the Finance → Savings tab, where it builds toward a goal over time instead of resetting each cycle. When you reach a goal the bar shows 100%+ and it’s marked “Target met”; nothing else changes automatically, so you decide whether to raise the goal or redirect contributions.
What are the green, amber and red icons on my categories?
They show how much of each category’s budget is used: a green tick means on track, amber means you’ve used most of it (each category has its own warning point, roughly 85–95% of its budget), and red means you’ve gone over.
How do I add or remove a category?
In Settings, scroll to “Add a new category”, give it a name and a group (Needs, Wants or Savings). To remove one, tap the 🗑 next to it. If it has spending this cycle you’ll be warned first; the transactions aren’t deleted, they just show as “Uncategorized”.
How do bills and recurring transactions work?
Settings → Bills & subscriptions lets you list a bill with its due day; a reminder banner appears on the Dashboard when one is due within 5 days. Recurring transactions (ticked “Repeats every cycle” when logging) are logged again automatically when a new cycle starts, and you can stop any of them under Settings → Recurring transactions.
What’s the eye icon at the top for?
Privacy mode. It hides your figures when someone is nearby; tap it again to show them.

Debts, loans & write-offs

What’s the difference between the Debt tracker and the “Debt repayment” category?
The Debt repayment category is a budget line, like Rent or Food — it caps what you plan to spend on debt this cycle. The Debt tracker (Finance tab) is more detailed: it tracks each creditor, how much you owe and what you’ve paid. Every payment you log there also counts toward the Debt repayment category, so the two stay in sync.
What happens when I add a debt?
The app asks what the borrowed money was for: add it to my balance (it’s recorded as income you can spend), I’ve already spent it (recorded as income plus a categorised expense, so the books balance), or just track the debt (no change to your balance — for example if someone else is holding the money).
I owe the same person more than once (say Grandma) — how should I record it?
Add each as its own debt. Debts owed to the same name (capitalisation doesn’t matter) are grouped under one header showing the combined total, amount paid and amount left, while each keeps its own reason and payment history. Typing a name you already owe shows how much you owe them across how many debts. If you’d rather have a single balance, tap “Merge into one debt” on the group: amounts, reasons and every payment are combined and nothing is lost, but you’ll no longer see them split by reason. To correct a wrong amount or reason on one debt, tap its Edit button instead.
I logged a debt payment twice (or wrongly). How do I fix it?
Tap the debt to see its payment history, then tap Edit beside the payment to change its amount or date, or delete it. This works for the current cycle and for archived cycles — for an archived one, that cycle’s totals and the debt’s paid-so-far are corrected too, so your balance stays right.
What does “Write off” do?
A write-off cancels some or all of what’s left without any money moving. Use it on a debt when the person you owe forgives or cancels it; use it on a loan when you choose to forgive what someone owes you or accept you won’t get it back. The cancelled part stops counting as owed, your balance and spending don’t change, nothing is added to your income, and the payments already logged stay on record. The card shows the written-off amount and date. You can write off part of the balance, and “Undo write-off” puts it back.
How do loans I’ve made to other people (Lent Out) work?
Add a loan with who it’s to, why and how much. When they repay, tap “Log repayment”: getting your own money back isn’t income, so only anything repaid beyond the amount lent is added to your extra income. Tap a loan to see its repayment history. The ✎ button records a repayment you received in an earlier cycle that was never logged.
How does the Avalanche / Snowball / Manual strategy work?
Avalanche highlights the debt with the highest interest rate as your focus — it saves the most money. Snowball highlights the smallest remaining balance — it clears debts fastest and builds momentum. Manual lets you pick any debt yourself. Switching strategy doesn’t change your debts, only which one is suggested as your current focus. Settled and written-off debts are never suggested.

Cycles, reports & history

What happens when a cycle is archived?
The cycle’s totals are saved to History, savings contributions fold into your lifetime savings totals, debt payments fold into each debt’s paid-so-far, a report is generated, and transactions and extra income reset for the next cycle. Category budgets and goals stay the same. You can archive manually from Settings at any time (“Archive this cycle & start fresh”).
Does the cycle roll over by itself?
For Salary accounts, yes: the app archives your cycle automatically at the end of payday (shortly before midnight, Nigeria time) and, if an email address is set in Settings and email delivery is set up for the app, sends you that cycle’s report. If the app was closed, opening it after payday starts the new cycle automatically, usually within a few minutes. Wages accounts never archive automatically.
Can I see a report for a cycle I already archived?
Yes. Go to History, scroll to “Past cycles” and tap View. It rebuilds the full report from the snapshot saved at archive time. Cycles archived before reports were saved show totals only. Next to each cycle there’s also a Restore button that brings an archived cycle back as the current one (needs an internet connection).
What’s the Year-end review?
Also in History: pick a year and it combines every cycle archived that year into one report — total income, spending and savings, a category breakdown, and spend per cycle.
Can I download, copy or email my report?
Yes. “Copy text” copies a plain-text version. “Download PDF” makes a formatted statement with charts. “Email it” opens your email app with the report pre-filled for the address set in Settings; you still have to press send yourself. Reports include a record-integrity section showing when entries were created and edited (edited ones are marked *), and a note that they are not official bank records.
What’s the shareable link?
On a report, “Create shareable link (7 days)” makes a read-only snapshot anyone can open without logging in. It expires after 7 days, and you can revoke it sooner under Settings → Shared links.
Can I share my budget with someone else, or let them use the app?
Anyone can use the app: send them the app’s link and they create their own account, with their own private budget. There is no shared or joint budget — each account is separate. To let someone see your figures without an account, send a shareable report link.

Signing in, security & privacy

Is my data private?
Your budget belongs to your account. Row-level security in the database keeps other people who sign up from ever seeing your categories, transactions, debts or loans, and regular use of the app never exposes one person’s data to another. As with any hosted service, the app’s operator administers the database and retains standard administrative access to the infrastructure it runs on.
How can I sign in?
With your email and password, or — once you’ve set them up in Settings → Security — a 6-digit PIN or your phone’s fingerprint / face unlock. On the login screen, switch between Password, PIN and Biometrics with the tabs. Your password always works. PIN and biometrics are conveniences for getting back in on a device that already has your login saved, so a brand-new device always needs your password first.
What’s the difference between “Lock app now” and “Sign out completely”?
If you’ve set a PIN or biometrics, “Lock app now” hides your data and keeps your login on this device so you can get back in with any method. “Sign out completely” ends your login on the server and clears it from the device — use it before handing your phone to someone or on a device you don’t trust. Without a PIN or biometrics, “Lock app now” signs you out. The app also locks itself after 5 minutes of inactivity.
What if I forget my PIN or password?
After 5 wrong PINs, or if you tap “Forgot?” on the PIN screen, the saved login is discarded and you sign in with your password; then set a new PIN in Settings (you don’t need the old one). For a forgotten password, tap “Forgot password?” on the login screen and follow the link emailed to you.
Why do I sometimes see a verification check when signing in?
It’s a quick bot check (Cloudflare Turnstile) on sign-in, sign-up and password reset that keeps automated scripts out. If it never appears, an ad or content blocker may be hiding it — allow challenges.cloudflare.com or try another browser.
How do daily reminders work?
Settings → Daily reminder: switch it on, pick a time and choose push notification, email or both. If you haven’t logged anything that day by then, you get a nudge to log an expense; on days you’ve already logged something, no reminder is sent. Push needs your permission on each device (tap “Allow push notifications”); email goes to the report email address saved in Settings (so make sure one is set).

Sync, offline & multiple devices

Does the app work offline?
Yes. Once loaded, you can keep logging, editing and deleting expenses with no connection. Changes are saved on your device and sync automatically when you’re back online — the sync indicator at the top shows whether everything has been saved. Debts and loans you add offline are also sent once you’re connected. A few things do need a connection: your first sign-in on a device, live exchange rates, the AI assistant, creating shareable links, restoring an archived cycle and password reset.
Can I use the same account on two devices?
Yes — sign in on each. If you changed things on two devices before they synced, the app keeps the new entries from both. In the rare case it has to choose between two edits to the very same entry, a copy of this device’s version is saved under Settings → Sync so you can download it and re-enter anything that looks off.
Light or dark mode — does it matter?
Purely a preference, from the header icon or Settings. Nothing about calculations or storage changes.

AI assistant & notes

How does the AI assistant work, and is my data used for training?
Tap the floating ✦ button. It sends your current cycle’s numbers (income, spending, categories, a summary of your debts) and your question to an AI service through the app’s server (currently Groq) and shows you the answer. Your conversation is saved in your account so it’s still there next time; the 🗑 icon in the assistant clears it. Whether the AI provider trains on requests depends on that provider’s own policy. The assistant runs on a shared free allowance, so it can occasionally be unavailable when many people are using it.
What’s the “My own notes” box for?
A private scratchpad at the bottom of this tab for your own budgeting rules and reminders. It saves automatically a second after you stop typing.

Troubleshooting

I got “could not save” or “unable to reach” — what do I do?
Check your internet connection. Your changes are kept on the device and retried automatically, so nothing is lost; the sync indicator turns back to saved once they get through. If it persists, refresh the page, or sign out and back in. Settings → About shows the app version, which helps if you need to report a problem.
I updated or fixed something but the app looks the same.
Close the app completely and reopen it (or refresh the page) so your device picks up the latest version. The version number is in Settings → About.
Payday has passed but my Dashboard still shows the old cycle.
Salary accounts roll over automatically — opening the app after payday should start the new cycle within a few minutes. If it doesn’t, check you’re online and refresh. Wages accounts never roll over automatically: use “Archive this cycle & start fresh” in Settings.
I run my own copy of the app. What must be set up?
Put your Supabase project URL and anon key in config.js (Project Settings → API); a console error naming a domain like “your-project-ref.supabase.co” means it still holds a placeholder. Deploy the api Edge Function with JWT verification off (the function checks sign-in itself), run schema.sql, and register the scheduled jobs in cron.sql. Email reports, reminders and push each need their own keys set as secrets on the function.

Widely-cited starting points — not rules. Adjust to your situation, especially if your income is contract-based or irregular.

Notes & strategy

01
Treat irregular or contract income as temporary. If your pay isn't guaranteed long-term, avoid raising fixed costs to match a good month.
02
Build the emergency fund before extra investing. Aim for 3–6 months of essential costs somewhere liquid.
03
Log expenses the same day. The categories and pace alerts only stay useful if the data is current.
04
If your total budgeted exceeds your income, something has to give. Check the "Total budgeted" line on Dashboard and Settings — it turns red the moment your categories add up to more than you earn.
05
Review every payday. Archive the cycle, read the generated report, and adjust next cycle's budgets based on what actually happened.
