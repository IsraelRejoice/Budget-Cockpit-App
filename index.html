Guide & FAQ

How this app works

How do I get started?
Go to Settings, enter your income, your payday (day of month), then set a budget for each category. Everything else updates automatically as you log expenses.
Why does my payday move around?
Your pay date is set to a fixed day of the month (default 25th). If that date falls on a Saturday or Sunday, the cycle automatically shifts to the preceding Friday — matching how most salaries are actually paid.
Why aren't Savings/Emergency Fund counted as spending?
Money you deliberately move into savings isn't an expense — it's still yours, just earmarked. It's excluded from "Spent" and tracked separately on the Savings tab, where it accumulates toward a goal over time instead of resetting each cycle.
What's "Safe to spend per day"?
(Remaining budget) ÷ (days left until payday). It's a pace check — if you spend at or below this amount each remaining day, you'll land within budget by payday. Spending more than this on a given day just means tighter days ahead, not an emergency.
What happens when I archive a cycle?
Totals are saved to History, savings contributions fold into your lifetime accumulated total, a report is generated, and transactions/extra income reset. Category budgets and goals stay the same.
Can I share this app with someone else?
If you send them your HTML file as-is, they'd be reading and writing to your same Google Sheet — their data would mix into yours, not stay separate, and you would not get a distinct report for them. For someone else to use this properly, they need their own copy: their own Google Sheet, their own Apps Script deployment, and their own token pasted into their own copy of this file.
How do I edit or delete an expense I logged wrong?
Tap any transaction row to edit it — the amount, category, method, description, and date can all be changed, and Save updates it in place instead of creating a duplicate. On mobile you can also swipe a row left to reveal quick Edit and Delete buttons. The ✕ button next to a transaction deletes it directly.
What are payment methods for, and do I have to use them?
Optional. Tagging an expense as Cash, Card, USSD, Mobile Wallet, or Bank Transfer doesn't change any calculations — it's just there so your transaction list (and any export) shows how money left your account, useful for spotting patterns like "I overspend when paying by card."
I received income in a different currency (e.g. dollars) — how does that get added to my Naira total?
When logging Extra Income, pick the currency you actually received it in. The app fetches a live exchange rate at that moment (via the Frankfurter rate API, which sources European Central Bank reference rates) and converts it to your home currency automatically — you'll see a preview like "$50 ≈ ₦77,500 at today's rate" before saving. The original amount, currency, the exact rate used, and the date are all kept on the entry so you can see exactly how it was converted later. Only your home-currency total ever counts toward your budget — nothing is double-counted.
How do I add or remove a category?
In Settings, scroll to "Add a new category" — give it a name and a group (Needs/Wants/Savings). To remove one, tap the 🗑 next to it in the category list. If it still has spending logged this cycle, you'll be warned first; the transactions themselves aren't deleted, they just show as "Uncategorized."
What's the difference between the Debt tracker and the "Debt repayment" category?
The Debt repayment category is a budget line, like Rent or Food — it caps how much you plan to spend on debt this cycle. The Debt tracker (in the Finance tab) is more granular: it tracks individual creditors, how much you owe each one, and lets you log payments per-creditor. Every debt payment you log there also counts toward the Debt repayment category automatically, so the two stay in sync.
How does the Avalanche/Snowball/Manual strategy work?
Avalanche automatically highlights whichever debt has the highest interest rate as your focus — mathematically saves you the most money. Snowball highlights whichever has the smallest remaining balance — clears faster and builds momentum. Manual lets you pick any debt yourself regardless of the numbers. Switching strategies doesn't change your debts, only which one is suggested as your current focus.
What happens to a savings goal once I hit it?
Nothing automatic — the progress bar just shows 100%+ and the category is marked "Target met." You decide whether to raise the goal, redirect contributions elsewhere, or leave it as a maintained buffer.
Can I see a report for a cycle I already archived?
Yes — go to History, scroll to "Past cycles," and tap "View" on any archived cycle. It rebuilds the full report (charts included) from the transaction snapshot saved at archive time. If a cycle was archived before this feature existed, you'll see totals only, with a note explaining why.
What's the Year-end review?
Also in History — pick a year and it aggregates every cycle archived in that year into one report: total income/spent/saved, a category breakdown across the whole year, and a chart of spend per cycle.
Can I download or email my report?
Both. "Copy text" copies a plain-text version. "Download PDF" generates a formatted document with charts. "Email it" opens your email app with the report pre-filled, addressed to whatever email you've set in Settings — you still have to hit send yourself, since a web page can't send email on its own.
How does the AI assistant work, and is my data used to train anything?
Tap the floating ✦ button. It sends your current cycle's numbers (income, spending, categories) plus your question to an AI provider you've connected yourself via Code.gs, and shows the answer back to you — nothing is stored by Anthropic or by this app beyond your own conversation history, which lives in your own backend. Whether the AI provider itself trains on the request depends on that provider's own policy — check current terms for whichever one you connect.
Where does my AI conversation history go?
It's saved in your own backend along with everything else, so it persists between visits. Use the 🗑 icon in the AI assistant header to clear it.
What's the "My own notes" box for?
A free-text scratchpad in the Guide tab for anything the structured features don't cover — personal rules, reminders, half-formed ideas. It autosaves a second after you stop typing.
Does this app work offline?
The app shell (layout, styling) loads instantly and works offline once installed via "Add to Home Screen." Your actual data — income, transactions, reports — still needs a live connection to reach your backend, so logging expenses while fully offline won't save until you're back online.
Light or dark mode — does it matter which I use?
Purely a preference, toggled from the header icon or Settings. Nothing about calculations, storage, or backend behavior changes between them.
I got a "could not save" or "unable to reach backend" message — what do I do?
First check you have a working internet connection. If it persists, confirm your Apps Script Web App is still deployed with "Anyone" access and that API_URL in the app matches your deployment's exec URL exactly. If you recently redeployed Apps Script, make sure you used "New version" under Manage deployments rather than creating a fresh deployment, which would change the URL.

General budgeting benchmarks

Widely-cited starting points — not rules. Adjust to your situation, especially if your income is contract-based or irregular.

Notes & strategy

01
Treat irregular or contract income as temporary. If your pay isn't guaranteed long-term, avoid raising fixed costs to match a good month.
02
Build the emergency fund before extra investing. Aim for 3–6 months of essential costs somewhere liquid.
03
Log expenses the same day. The categories and pace alerts only stay useful if the data is current.
04
If your total budgeted exceeds your income, something has to give. Check the "Total budgeted" line on Dashboard and Settings — it turns red the moment your categories add up to more than you earn.
05
Review every payday. Archive the cycle, read the generated report, and adjust next cycle's budgets based on what actually happened.
